How It Works

Everything you need to know before we speak — what the programme is, who it suits, what happens each week, and what to expect if your child joins.

01

What this actually is

I teach GCSE Maths online, in small groups of a maximum of eight students, to those working towards the Higher tier. Eight is a deliberate number — small enough that nobody is hidden, large enough that nobody is alone.

Your child joins a cohort grouped by year and by ability, and works through a structured programme of over 50 chapters with the same group each week. I assess that ability myself in a Discovery Session after a booked call — a short, low-pressure session with your child that shows me how they actually work and their general capabilities.

The chapter order has been carefully curated over a decade of teaching. Each one is precisely positioned to ensure the foundations are built from the ground up.

It isn't one-to-one tutoring, and it isn't a recorded course. It's a taught programme with a fixed curriculum, weekly homework, and someone following up when it isn't done.

02

Starting in different year groups

You can start at any point from Year 8 to Year 11 — but the timing makes a difference.

Year 8–9
The full programme, properly. Every topic covered in order, with time to revisit anything that hasn't landed. This is where the structure does the most work.
Early Year 10
Still comfortable. We move at a slightly faster pace, but everything gets covered.
Late Year 10
We prioritise. Some topics get less time than I would like, and I choose those based on what your child already has and where the marks are.
Year 11
A different programme. There isn't time to teach everything, so I focus on the topics that will move the grade most and on exam technique.

04

What a week looks like

The live lesson

One taught lesson a week with your child's cohort. New material, worked examples, and questions attempted with me there.

The Open Study Session

A second lesson each week, free, and optional — though I'd recommend it. Students can bring questions, work through homework with me on hand, or simply use it as a quiet hour where someone is available if they get stuck.

Homework

Set weekly and linked to what we covered, then broken down into a small, manageable number of questions spread across four or five days rather than done in one sitting.

Homework is sent back to me within two or three days rather than all at once, so I can check it through the week rather than only on the deadline.

There are two reasons for that. Firstly, if something has gone wrong, I catch it before the next lesson rather than after. Secondly, it stops work being crammed into the night before — which is the single biggest reason maths tutoring fails to move a grade.

Between lessons

Students can message me during the week. If they're stuck, they shouldn't sit on it until the next lesson — a week of confusion is a week of lost practice.

05

The resources

Teaching is only part of it. What a student does in the six days between lessons is what decides the grade, and most of them have no idea where to start. These are the tools that fix that.

01

The Progress Tracker

The central hub of independent study

A spreadsheet holding every chapter of the course, designed personally by me. Formulas calculate when each topic needs revisiting, so there's no guesswork — it tells your child what to work on next, every week, right up to the exam.

I'll show you the tracker on our call.

02

Detailed Notes

The full version, handwritten

A complete set of handwritten notes for each chapter, with worked examples covering each form the question takes.

The place to start again from scratch.

03

Cheat Sheets

One page per chapter

The method, the formulae and the mistakes that cost marks — designed to be glanced at rather than read.

A quick reminder on a chapter your child has already learned, not for learning it from scratch.

The ten minutes before a lesson, the night before a test, the morning of the exam.

I also teach them how to revise

This sounds obvious and it isn't. Most students have never actually been taught what revision is. They read through notes, highlight a page, feel like they've worked, and retain almost nothing — because none of that is revision.

So I teach it properly, with explanations and worked examples of what effective revision looks like in maths specifically. The tracker is the tool they use to put it into practice.

06

If your child misses a lesson

Every lesson is recorded and added to their archive the same week. They can watch it back in full and catch up properly rather than arriving at the next lesson a step behind.

The recordings stay available from the day they join right through to the end of their exams. So a topic covered in Year 9 is still there to revisit in the week before the paper.

07

The 40-lesson guarantee

I guarantee a minimum of 40 live lessons per academic year. In practice this can be more.

If I deliver fewer than 40 in a year, you don't lose them. I credit the shortfall against your next invoice on a pro-rata basis. You pay for what you receive.

08

Fees

Fees start from approximately £40 per lesson, depending on the payment plan you choose.

This covers everything.

We'll talk through the payment plans on our call, before the Discovery Session with your child, so we can settle on the one that works best for your family.

09

Exam boards

All boards are welcome, and it makes very little difference. At Higher tier the content is set nationally.

What does differ is how questions are worded and how papers are structured. So in the run-up to the exams, every student practises questions from their own board, so nothing on the day looks unfamiliar.

10

How you'll know it's working

You receive a written progress report every quarter, covering attendance, homework, and where your child is against the topics we've covered.

If something starts going wrong, you won't wait three months to hear about it. I'll contact you when it happens. You can also contact me at any point — you do not need to wait for a report or a scheduled call.

11

Who this isn't for

I'd rather tell you now than take your money and disappoint you.

  • Students working towards Foundation tier. Everything I teach is pitched at Higher, aiming for Grades 7 to 9. A student who isn't there yet would find the room discouraging rather than motivating.
  • Students who need intensive one-to-one support. A group of eight works brilliantly for students who are capable and need structure and push. It's the wrong setting for someone who needs constant individual attention.

If your child isn't right for the programme, I'll say so on our call and point you towards something that would suit them better. I don't take students I don't believe will benefit.

You have 14 days to change your mind. Because we arrange everything remotely, you have a statutory right to cancel within 14 days of signing and receive a full refund, for any reason. Confirmed in writing.
12

Safeguarding

I hold an Enhanced DBS check with the Children's Barred List, which is the highest level available and the appropriate one for anyone teaching children. The certificate is available on request.

Still have a question?

The quickest way to get an answer is a short call. No obligation, and I'll tell you honestly if the programme isn't the right fit for your child. There's nothing to lose by asking.
